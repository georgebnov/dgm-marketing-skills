# AI and privacy checks

Read this file at step 2 when AI made text or images, when the material collects personal data, when the site uses cookies, or when the business makes automated decisions about people.

## The state of AI law in Canada

- Canada has no laws specific to AI. Many existing laws and legal structures apply to AI.
- The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) did not pass. It died on the order paper when Parliament was dissolved. The Voluntary Code of Conduct on the Responsible Development and Management of Advanced Generative AI Systems is very high level on the advertising side.
- Canada does not require the business to disclose that an ad contains AI content or was made with AI.
- The EU requires AI disclosure. That EU rule came into force in August 2026.
- California and New York probably have disclosure rules for synthetic performers. Example: an AI-generated hand in a jewelry ad instead of a real hand.

## AI checks

| Check | Level if it fails |
|---|---|
| **False or misleading images.** Each AI-generated image of the product or service shows it accurately. Apply step 1 of SKILL.md to the image: is it false or misleading in a material respect to the credulous, inexperienced consumer in a rush? This risk is not new: photo editing existed long before AI. This is the biggest AI risk. | High |
| **Human review.** A human reviewed each AI-made ad, image, and claim before publishing. AI may make the material, but a person must check it before publishing. The advertiser remains responsible. "The AI made it" is not a legal defense. If you cannot see a review record: not checked, proof "the name of the reviewer and the review date for each AI-made item". | High |
| **Bias and representation.** People in AI images are representative. Examples of failure: a Government of Canada ad used an AI-generated image of an Inuit woman who did not look representative of Inuit women in Canada. A hockey team showed an AI-generated image of fans where all the fans were men. AI output carries bias. | Medium |
| **Accessibility.** People of all abilities can access the products and services. If AI made the access points, check that they consider all kinds of people. | Medium |
| **EU disclosure.** If the material reaches the EU, it discloses AI content. | Medium if it reaches the EU. Low (watch) if it stays in Canada. |
| **Synthetic performers.** If the material reaches California or New York and shows an AI-generated person or body part, flag the synthetic-performer disclosure rules. | Medium if it reaches California or New York. Low otherwise. |

## Privacy checks

Personal information is information about an identifiable individual. Digital marketing collects it. The use of AI is no excuse for non-compliant handling of personal information.

| Check | Level if it fails |
|---|---|
| The business collects personal information only for the purposes that it tells people. | Medium |
| The business does not over-collect. | Medium |
| People have the opportunity to withdraw their consent. | Medium |
| The business knows how its AI tools process personal information, and does not use or disclose it for other purposes. If you cannot see this: not checked, proof "a description of how each AI tool processes personal information". | Medium |
| **Quebec automated decisions.** If the business makes automated decisions about people (for example, from an AI determination of their profile), a Quebec law requires disclosure in advance. The person must also get the opportunity to discuss it. | High if it reaches Quebec |

## Cookie checks

- **Most of Canada.** No law requires a cookie notice specifically. Privacy law requires express consent only if the use goes beyond the reasonable expectation of the person.
  - Only essential cookies to run the website: no notice is necessary. Result: pass.
  - Additional cookies for a personalized experience, or cookies that can profile people: a cookie notice with opt-in for the additional uses is necessary. The short answer is: probably yes. Level if missing: Medium.
- **Quebec.** A statute (Law 25) requires it. On a digital platform that geolocates people or profiles them, those features must be off by default. Level if missing: Medium.
