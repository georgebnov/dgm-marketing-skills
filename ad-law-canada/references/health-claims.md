# Health claim checks

Read this file at step 2 when the product is food, a cosmetic, a drug, a natural health product, a cleaner, a personal care product, or when the material has a health benefit. Health Canada enforces these rules.

These rules add to the general rules. The Competition Act covers all products. The Consumer Protection Acts cover all products sold B2C. Some products also have specific regulation because of the type of product.

## Check 1: product category (High)

The ingredients and the claims decide the category, not how the product looks. Example: 2 lipsticks look the same. One is a cosmetic because of its ingredients and claims. The other is a drug because it contains a sunscreen.

1. List the ingredients that the user supplies, and every claim in the material.
2. Decide the category: cosmetic, natural health product (for example vitamins), OTC drug (over-the-counter drug), prescription drug, food, or cleaner.
3. If a claim or an ingredient moves the product into a more regulated category, that is a finding. Example: a sunscreen claim on a cosmetic.
4. If you cannot decide the category from the inputs, give "not checked" at High. Proof: "the product category and its notification, registration, or authorization".

## Check 2: product authorization or notification (High)

Most personal care products need a notification to the government (pre-notification or post-market notification) or a pre-market authorization.

- The range: prescription drugs need a very long registration process with Health Canada. Cosmetics are at the other end: the business must tell the government and submit some forms within 10 days of bringing the product to market.
- Therapeutic products (for example natural health products such as vitamins, and OTC drugs) usually need more complex licensing or registration.

Result: not checked unless the user shows the notification, the registration, or the authorization.

## Check 3: claims inside the authorization (High)

For a therapeutic product:

- Each claim must stay inside the product authorization. The authorization is a product monograph (the approved description of a product and what you can say about it) or a registration.
- Therapeutic claims must also be substantiated.
- Even if a claim is true and the business can substantiate it, it may not be allowed to say it. This happens if Canada did not authorize that claim for that ingredient. Example: emerging science can show a health benefit of an ingredient, but if Canada did not authorize that claim, the business cannot make it.
- The authorization wins over the proof. A claim outside the authorization is a fail, even with good proof.

Result: pass if each claim matches the monograph or the registration. Fail if a claim goes outside it.

## Check 4: cosmetic claims (High)

Cosmetic claims also need substantiation with "adequate and proper testing", done before publishing. Use the same result scale as step 1 of SKILL.md.

## Check 5: co-promotions (High)

If the business partners with a brand on a joint promotion, and the partner's product has more regulation, the promotion goes into that area. Run checks 1 to 4 on the partner's product too.
