# Green claim checks

Read this file at step 2 when the material has a claim about the environment, emissions, net zero, "natural", recyclable, or compostable. Every check here is High.

Green claims are a real enforcement priority. The laws are the Competition Act and the Canadian Environmental Protection Act (CEPA). The new rules signal heightened government interest in green claims. Consumers value green benefits, so the benefits help sell a product or service.

## The base test

The same general impression test applies: the credulous and technically inexperienced consumer in a rush, an ordinary person. The business needs substantiation for the general impression of the claim, not only for its literal words. Example: "100% eco-friendly" gives the impression "it does not harm the environment". The proof must cover that impression.

## Kinds of green claim

Classify each green claim. One claim can be more than 1 kind. Then the rules overlap, and all of them apply.

| Kind | Example | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Performance claim | "lasts for 50 washes" | Adequate and proper testing. This rule existed before the greenwashing changes. |
| Claim about a business or a business activity | "we are a net zero company", "our operations protect local rivers" | Adequate and proper testing or substantiation. Greenwashing prohibition in force since June 2024 (Competition Act). |
| Green claim about a product | "each bottle has 30% lower emissions" | Testing. Greenwashing prohibition in force since June 2024 (Competition Act). |
| Recyclability or compostability claim, or plastic packaging | "recyclable", "compostable" | CEPA: a plastics registry and draft rules on recyclability and compostability claims. Flag it for a lawyer to check the current CEPA rules. |

## The "adequate and proper" test

For each claim, check that the test:

1. Is reproducible. It does not need to be perfect.
2. Uses the conditions in which the consumer will use the product.
3. Was done before the business advertised the claim.

Result: pass if a test report meets all 3 points and covers the general impression. Partial if the report exists but misses 1 point (for example, lab conditions instead of consumer-use conditions). Not checked if no report was supplied. Proof: "a reproducible test report under consumer-use conditions, dated before publishing, that covers <general impression>". Fail if the test was done after publishing, or the report does not support the claim.

## Who enforces (for the "rule" line)

- The Competition Bureau enforces green claims under the Competition Act.
- The source has 2 statements on private enforcement. One statement says that the new private right of access lets any litigant (private citizens, public interest groups, competitors) bring a green claim to the Competition Tribunal for the same $10 million penalties. The other statement says that the new greenwashing provisions have no private right of action. Do not resolve this in the review. The level stays High either way.
- A misleading green claim also falls under the general prohibitions. So consumers can bring a class action. There were many class actions on consumer products for misleading environmental and "natural" claims.
