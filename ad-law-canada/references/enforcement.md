## Who enforces what

- **Competition Bureau** (federal) enforces the general prohibition, the pricing tests, greenwashing, performance claims, and contest mini rules. It can ask the Competition Tribunal for a penalty up to the greater of $10 million ($15 million for a repeat order) and 3 times the benefit, or 3% of annual worldwide gross revenue when the benefit cannot be determined, and a rescission-style order.
- **Private access since June 2025.** Private litigants can sue for (almost) any civil false or misleading advertising if the application is in the "public interest". They do not have to prove damages. The money goes to the Receiver General. Another source statement says that the new greenwashing provisions have no private right of action.
- **CRTC** enforces CASL (email and SMS) and telemarketing.
- **Privacy Commissioners**: federal, Quebec, Alberta, and British Columbia. Only Alberta, Quebec, and BC have their own private-sector privacy statute. Elsewhere PIPEDA applies.
- **Health Canada** enforces food, cosmetics, drugs, natural health products, cleaners, and similar products.
- **Consumer Protection Acts** (B2C only): damages for a misleading representation before the purchase. Rescission and damages if the contract did not comply (for example, internet agreements that did not tell consumers the required things). Both are possible as class actions.
- **Criminal Code**: a contest that does not comply can be an illegal lottery.

## Self-regulation (Low)

Self-regulation is not law. Industry wrote its own rules so that the government does not have to regulate it.

- The Canadian Code of Advertising Standards (Ad Standards) applies to all advertisers in all media in Canada, even if the business is not a member of Ad Standards. Consumers and industry groups can complain under it, and an adjudication process follows. Advertisers can also use it as a lower-cost way to challenge a competitor ad.
- Broadcasters require Ad Standards preclearance for TV and other broadcast ads in some regulated fields. Example: a food company with a TV ad gets preclearance from Ad Standards, then the ad goes to Telecaster, the preclearance service of the broadcasters.
- Ad Standards has extra self-regulation for broadcast advertising to children, gaming advertising, alcohol advertising, and health product advertising.
- Industry codes can also apply: for example Spirits Canada (alcohol), Beer Canada (beer), and the Canadian Marketing Association.
