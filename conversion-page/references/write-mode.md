# Write mode detail

Read this file at step W1 of `SKILL.md`. It holds the questions, the claim structures, and the landing page order. The CTA test variables are in the "CTA cheat sheet" of `references/checklists.md`.

## W1. The questions

Ask for all of these in 1 message:

1. The offer and the 1 conversion event.
2. The user group, and the words that customers use for their problem (from interviews, support tickets, Reddit, sales calls).
3. The traffic source and the exact ad copy, if it is a campaign page.
4. What happens if the customer does not act, today. Keep asking until the answer is a specific consequence in their life (for example "a flooded basement after the next storm", "a missed tax deadline in 3 weeks").
5. What you do that the incumbent does not, in 1 sentence.
6. Power: years of experience, number of installs, patents, a method, ingredients, certifications.
7. Proof: review rating and count, case studies with results, customer logos, press, awards, statistics. Exact numbers only.
8. Brand cues: colors, a border, a filter, corner style, tone (professional, fun, tech-forward).
9. Images that exist: product photos, photos of people, lifestyle shots.
10. The minimum form fields, and what happens after the visitor submits.
11. Product page only: brand, product, format, category, variant, color, weight, size, specs, top customer questions.

## W2. The claim

Draft 6 claims, 1 per structure below. Keep only claims that the user can stand behind.

- **Exemption**: the best result without the usual cost. Template: "The <best quality> <product> without <usual pain>."
- **Absolution**: the pain ends for good. Template: "Never <pain> again."
- **Exponential**: a multiple of the result. Template: "<N>x more <result>."
- **Accretion**: a new ability at last. Template: "Finally, a <product> that <new ability>."
- **Acceleration**: the result in a short time. Template: "<Result> in <N> <days or weeks>."
- **Superlative**: the best way to the result. Template: "The <fastest, easiest, cheapest> way to <result>."

A claim with a number needs that number from W1. Example of a feature heading rewritten as a claim: "Cloud-based inventory management software for retailers" becomes "Count your stock in 10 minutes, not 2 hours." Clarity beats cleverness. Write the benefit, not the feature: "a clean cut every time", not "stainless steel blade".

## W3. The landing page order

Read the matching section of `references/checklists.md` for the element rules. For a landing page, use this order:

1. Nav and logo at the top, with a sticky CTA. For a paid campaign page, strip the nav. Each nav link is an exit from the conversion.
2. Heading: the best claim. It matches the ad.
3. Supporting copy: 1 short statement of the main benefit, with the keywords of the ad.
4. Hero image brief: a non-generic, fast-loading image that passes the squint test, ideally with a person who looks toward the CTA, with 1 brand cue.
5. Primary CTA above the fold: an action verb plus the value ("Get your free inventory audit").
6. Floors, with the CTA repeated every few floors: key benefits (bullets and icons), how it works, power, then proof.
7. The form: the minimum fields, plus 1 line that says what happens next ("We will call you within 1 business day with your estimate").

For the other types, use the section list of the type in `references/checklists.md`.
